Default effect is easy -  people tend to stick with a pre-selected option, rather than choosing an alternative

Story time....

In October 2016, a big chunk of the internet stopped working for a few hours. Twitter, Netflix, Reddit, Spotify and PayPal were hard to reach, mostly in the US. You’d expect a mastermind behind it. It was a program called Mirai, and its trick was guessing passwords. Its code carried a list of 61 factory logins, and the list was embarrassingly simple. “admin” and “admin.” “root” and “12345.” It tried them on home routers, security cameras and DVRs, and they worked, *Seriously*. It infected hundreds of thousands of devices. Each one kept doing its normal job while secretly taking orders from the attacker(botnet). Then they all flooded one company, Dyn, which runs part of the internet’s phonebook (DNS - Domain name system). With the phonebook down, your phone couldn’t look up where Netflix lived, even though Netflix itself was fine. Nobody got outsmarted. The attackers jiggled the handle and the door was open.

So why didn’t anyone change the password? Blame your *brain*. Changing a setting means finding it, understanding it and deciding something, and your brain hates all three. Doing nothing costs nothing, so it wins(lazy excuse). We also assume the default was chosen by someone who knew best(because we don't care about tech), when really it was chosen so the device would just work out of the box. Psychologists call this the *default effect*, and the most famous proof is organ donation. Yeah

In a study, Germany, where you have to *opt in,* had a donor sign-up rate of about 12%. While in Austria, where you’re a donor unless you *opt out,* had almost 100%. Two neighboring countries, similar people, similar values, and one pre-ticked box made the difference. Whoever sets the default decides for almost everyone. Set it to “admin” and you get a botnet made of doorbells. Set it to a unique password and the same lazy human ends up safe without trying. 

Be secure by default.....
